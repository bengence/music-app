# Major Scales Reference

## What are Major Scales?

Major scales are the foundation of Western music theory. They consist of seven notes following a specific pattern of whole and half steps: **W-W-H-W-W-W-H** (where W = whole step, H = half step).

## The 12 Major Scales

### C Major
**Notes:** C - D - E - F - G - A - B
- **Key Signature:** No sharps or flats
- **Pattern:** C-D-E-F-G-A-B-C

### G Major  
**Notes:** G - A - B - C - D - E - F#
- **Key Signature:** 1 sharp (F#)
- **Pattern:** G-A-B-C-D-E-F#-G

### D Major
**Notes:** D - E - F# - G - A - B - C#
- **Key Signature:** 2 sharps (F#, C#)
- **Pattern:** D-E-F#-G-A-B-C#-D

### A Major
**Notes:** A - B - C# - D - E - F# - G#
- **Key Signature:** 3 sharps (F#, C#, G#)
- **Pattern:** A-B-C#-D-E-F#-G#-A

### E Major
**Notes:** E - F# - G# - A - B - C# - D#
- **Key Signature:** 4 sharps (F#, C#, G#, D#)
- **Pattern:** E-F#-G#-A-B-C#-D#-E

### B Major
**Notes:** B - C# - D# - E - F# - G# - A#
- **Key Signature:** 5 sharps (F#, C#, G#, D#, A#)
- **Pattern:** B-C#-D#-E-F#-G#-A#-B

### F# Major
**Notes:** F# - G# - A# - B - C# - D# - E#
- **Key Signature:** 6 sharps (F#, C#, G#, D#, A#, E#)
- **Pattern:** F#-G#-A#-B-C#-D#-E#-F#

### F Major
**Notes:** F - G - A - Bb - C - D - E
- **Key Signature:** 1 flat (Bb)
- **Pattern:** F-G-A-Bb-C-D-E-F

### Bb Major
**Notes:** Bb - C - D - Eb - F - G - A
- **Key Signature:** 2 flats (Bb, Eb)
- **Pattern:** Bb-C-D-Eb-F-G-A-Bb

### Eb Major
**Notes:** Eb - F - G - Ab - Bb - C - D
- **Key Signature:** 3 flats (Bb, Eb, Ab)
- **Pattern:** Eb-F-G-Ab-Bb-C-D-Eb

### Ab Major
**Notes:** Ab - Bb - C - Db - Eb - F - G
- **Key Signature:** 4 flats (Bb, Eb, Ab, Db)
- **Pattern:** Ab-Bb-C-Db-Eb-F-G-Ab

### Db Major
**Notes:** Db - Eb - F - Gb - Ab - Bb - C
- **Key Signature:** 5 flats (Bb, Eb, Ab, Db, Gb)
- **Pattern:** Db-Eb-F-Gb-Ab-Bb-C-Db

## Scale Degrees

Each note in a major scale has a specific function:
1. **Tonic** - The home note
2. **Supertonic** - Above the tonic
3. **Mediant** - Midway between tonic and dominant
4. **Subdominant** - Below the dominant
5. **Dominant** - The most important note after tonic
6. **Submediant** - Below the mediant
7. **Leading Tone** - Leads back to tonic

## Practice Tips

1. **Start with C Major** - No sharps or flats make it easiest to learn
2. **Use the Circle of Fifths** - Learn scales in the order they appear
3. **Practice ascending and descending** - Both directions are important
4. **Practice with a metronome** - Start slow and gradually increase tempo
5. **Learn scale patterns on your instrument** - Memorize fingerings for each scale
